## 2. Common mistakes / red flags

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Not talking about the team enough; getting enamored with idea/product | No conviction that *this team* wins | Fareed |
| Spending 5+ min walking through a demo without explaining why the problem matters | Doesn't communicate the problem or stakes | Emily |
| Overblown TAM ("every man, woman, and child wants my SaaS") | Reads as unserious; reviewers already know the market exists | Marcus |
| No clear differentiation in a crowded category | Forces reviewer to do the differentiation work themselves | Kenan |
| Listing competitors via Harvey Balls / feature grid | Not real competitive analysis; doesn't show idea-maze depth | Josh |
| Rambling / unfocused responses | Brevity = clarity; if you can't compress, your deck isn't ready | Marcus |
| Burying the lede (great traction at the bottom of the list) | Reviewer might not get there | Sam |
| Decks longer than 7-10 slides; small font | Reviewer won't click through; signals lack of editing | Josh / Sam / Marcus |
| Typos in the application | Bad attention to detail; everything is checkable now | Sam |
| Missing or empty LinkedIn profile | Hard to assess background; reviewers won't dig through posts | Marcus |
| Omitting outlier signals (YouTube, side projects, weird achievements) | Founders are outliers; hiding the outlier evidence undersells you | Troy / Fareed |
| Wasting interview time introducing all six team members or an advisor | The 10-min slot is a movie trailer, every word matters | Marcus |
| Founders cutting each other off in interviews; awkward silences on "who takes this" | Bad founder chemistry signal | Josh |
| Technical founder says "we'll just build it ourselves" when asked about hiring | Hasn't studied company-building, just product-building | Troy |
| Long-winded / vague when describing yourselves | Reviewers can't extract the brag-worthy specifics | Andrew |
| Insider founders who don't ask enough questions of other companies | Falls in love with industry, doesn't pursue truth | Marcus |
| Founders who over-productize in customer calls (lead with a solution) | Misses root problem; not actively listening | Emily |
| Anchoring on a market-map / 2x2 / "look how big this category is" framing | By the time a market is legible, valuable entry points have closed | Emily Bennett |
| Building "one more feature" before talking to users | Should pick 10 users and go deep, not broaden product | Emily Bennett |
| AI-distilled responses with no personal framing or insight | Delivery can be AI-assisted; insight must come from you | Emily |

## 4. Communication framework (deck + interview)

**Fareed's Minto Pyramid — SCQA framing for the opening:**

| Element | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **S**ituation | What's the overview / current state of the world? |
| **C**omplication | What problem does that create? |
| **Q**uestion | The implicit "how do we solve this?" |
| **A**nswer | Your solution / company |

**Bottom-line-up-front:** the most important thing in the first three sentences. If those aren't strong, the reviewer is out.

**The 1-sentence pitch** must be produceable on the fly, from multiple angles, in response to different questions. Inability to compress = founder-quality concern.

## 6. Ideas, validation, and "earned insight"

| Stage | What's needed |
|---|---|
| **Why this idea** | Process by which you arrived at it; why you're the right team |
| **Validation > small traction** | A few customers with no clear next step is worse than wide market validation without revenue |
| **Insider vs outsider** | Outsiders must show "I did the work" (deep research, customer conversations, mapped industry). Insiders must answer "why doesn't this exist already, given that you know the market?" |
| **Idea-maze depth** | Understanding every competitor, why each will fail; why your insight is the correct one others have missed |
| **Listening** | Root problem before solution; resist over-productizing in customer calls |
| **Market knowledge** | Who succeeded, who failed, why; map of all players you've contacted |

**Validation signals (from soft to hard):**

1. Customers responding warmly to outreach (vs having to beg for a meeting)
2. Customers introducing you to their friends without being asked
3. Soft verbal interest from a wide customer set
4. Hard verbal interest / LOI
5. Short-term revenue contracts
6. Long-term recurring revenue with significant companies

## 7. Emily Bennett's "early market" framework

Markets begin as **behavioral shifts in small, overlooked communities** — before any market map can be drawn.

**What to look for (in your own product or the market you're entering):**

| Signal | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Pre-launch intent** | Waitlist with organic sharing, people bringing others in unasked |
| **Self-organizing community** | Discussion forms around the product before it's live, founders don't have to prop it up |
| **User-created content** | Explainers, tutorials, threads appear before official documentation |
| **High intensity from a small group** | Handful of users spending unusual time with the product because they want to |
| **Tool replacement** | Users abandon prior tools |
| **Use-case bending** | Users find applications the founders didn't plan |

**Building method:** pick 10 users (or fewer), go unreasonably deep on how they work and what they need. Narrow-then-broaden beats build-then-pray. Insights from 10 power users tend to generalize to the eventual 10,000.

**The trap:** waiting for a market to be legible / mapped / labeled. By then the entry points have closed.

## 8. Andrew Chen's FAQ answers (key clarifications)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| How do I know if I'm cut out to be a founder? | Most people already know. The question is *when*, not *whether*. |
| Am I too early to apply (not incorporated)? | Pre-incorporation is the sweet spot. ~12 of 70 in last batch hadn't quit their jobs at application time. |
| Do I need a cofounder? | No, but it's a signal. Convincing one smart person to join is step-one validation. |
| Background requirements? | No checklist. Looking for *unique* — could be 18-year-old skipping college or cofounder of a $1B+ company. Pedigree is one signal, not the only one. |
| AI companies only? | No, horizontal. B2B, B2C, infra/dev tools, healthcare, gaming, govtech, robotics, AI all welcome. Pivots welcome too. |
| Outside the US? | Yes. Visa support offered. Founders apply from US, India, UK, Nigeria, Canada, dozens of other countries. |
| Move to SF? | Required for the 12-week program. After, plenty go back home; plenty stay. |
| Biggest application mistake? | Being long-winded and vague when talking about *yourselves*. Bury the lede on the team. Brag with specifics. |
| What if my company doesn't work out? | Relationship is lifelong, not batch-long. Speedrun re-backs founders on second ideas and pairs them across the network. |

## 9. "Brag with specifics" — the writing standard

When describing yourself, use **specific facts and figures**, not adjectives:

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "Successful prior founder" | "Took company from $0 → $X million ARR in Y months" |
| "Worked on a well-known product" | "Served Z million monthly users at [product]" |
| "Built a popular side project" | "Hit 1M downloads / 10K GitHub stars / 100K subscribers" |
| "Technical background" | "Shipped infrastructure used by N teams" / "Open-sourced X with Y adopters" |
| "Helped scale a team" | "Hired and led team of N engineers" |

Andrew calls this "The Dinner Party Jerk Test" — the only good way to pitch a team in writing.

## 10. Pre-submission checklist

- [ ] Deck: 6–10 slides, large font, BLUF in first 3 sentences
- [ ] Application opens with SCQA structure
- [ ] 1-sentence pitch tested for clarity, multiple framings rehearsed
- [ ] LinkedIn profiles up to date with specific responsibilities (not just titles)
- [ ] Twitter / GitHub / Substack / YouTube linked if they show insight or build-in-public
- [ ] Outlier signals included (weird projects, unusual achievements, off-the-beaten-path work)
- [ ] Team section: who you are, why this team wins, specific numbers
- [ ] Traction section: highest quality of traction front-and-center, not buried
- [ ] Validation section: customer conversation count, mapped industry players
- [ ] Idea-maze: who competitors are, why each will fail
- [ ] Tangible plan: next 3-month and 12-month priorities with metrics + hires
- [ ] Differentiation made explicit ("we are different from X because…") — don't make reviewer do it
- [ ] Zero typos (especially given AI tooling makes this easy)
- [ ] AI used for tightening, but personal framing/insight intact
- [ ] If solo founder: who you're recruiting is explicit
- [ ] If outsider: evidence of the work (experts contacted, market knowledge, customer conversations)
- [ ] If insider: clear answer to "why doesn't this exist already?"
