# Battle Card: Public.com vs Gordon / General Liquidity
*Skill: startup-competitors | Generated: 2026-04-16*

---

## Who They Are

Public.com is a US-based retail investment platform that launched "the world's first Agentic Brokerage" on March 31, 2026. [Data] They are best known as an investing app for US equities with a social layer. Their agentic move is recent and positioned as a full rebrand of their execution model — from brokerage to "agent that trades on your behalf." They are backed by institutional investors and have meaningful retail distribution, particularly among younger US equity investors. Galaxy Digital's NickAI launched around the same time in a different direction (visual workflow builder).

**Key claim:** "Agentic Brokerage" — the agent executes your trades.

**What they actually have (honest assessment):**
- US equities execution via their own custodial platform [Data]
- AI-assisted research layer [Knowledge-Based]
- Some form of execution delegation via agent interface [Data]
- No crypto exchange integration [Data]
- No plan-first preview (no published evidence of this capability) [Data]
- No multi-mode permission model (no published evidence) [Data]
- No onchain support [Data]

---

## Strengths (be honest)

- **Distribution:** Public.com has an existing retail user base. They are not starting from zero. [Data]
- **Brand trust:** As a custodial brokerage, they carry the implied trust of holding customer assets. Operators who are US equities-focused may find the custody model reassuring. [Knowledge-Based]
- **PR velocity:** Their "Agentic Brokerage" launch generated significant press. They are ahead on category narrative in the equities-only lane. [Data]
- **Regulatory familiarity:** As a registered broker-dealer (or similar), they have already navigated the compliance path that Gordon's multi-venue model must eventually address. [Knowledge-Based]
- **Simpler onboarding:** Single-venue, custodial model means less setup friction for a user who only wants to trade US equities. [Estimate]

---

## Weaknesses

- **US equities only:** No crypto, no onchain, no international venues. [Data]
- **No plan-first preview:** Execution delegation without a structured "here is what I'm about to do" confirmation step is the exact failure mode operators fear. [Data]
- **No permission granularity:** No published multi-mode permission model. The operator cannot enforce "ask me before every order" vs. "auto-execute within these parameters." [Data]
- **Custodial model creates lock-in:** Operators who want to keep assets at their existing brokers (Schwab, IBKR) cannot use Public.com without moving assets. [Knowledge-Based]
- **Single venue = single context:** No cross-venue portfolio intelligence. The operator with Robinhood + Coinbase + a brokerage account cannot consolidate their view. [Data]
- **No backtesting loop:** Research leads to execution, but there is no thesis validation layer. [Knowledge-Based]
- **No onchain:** Entirely absent from the DeFi/onchain segment. [Data]

## How to Win Against Them

**Lead with venue breadth + context unity.**
"Public.com can trade your stocks for you. Gordon can manage your Binance, your Coinbase, your Schwab, and your Solana wallet — all with one thesis, one permission model, and a plan preview before any order fires."

**Lead with trust architecture.**
"When an AI agent is about to execute a trade, what's the confirmation step? With Public.com, we don't know. With Gordon, you see a structured diff: here are the orders, here's the rationale, here are the parameters. You approve or reject before anything hits a venue."

**Lead with the conviction-preservation frame.**
"Public.com replaces your judgment with an agent's. Gordon amplifies your judgment by enforcing your rules. You keep the thesis. Gordon enforces the discipline."

**Use the multi-venue comparison directly.**
For any operator who has crypto + stocks: "Public.com can't see half your portfolio. Gordon sees all of it."

## When They Win

- The operator only has US equities and wants the simplest possible AI-assisted trading experience
- The operator wants a custodial model (doesn't want to connect an existing brokerage)
- The operator is new to investing and wants a guided, simplified interface (Gordon's terminal metaphor assumes operator-grade sophistication)
- The operator trusts Public.com's brand and is not interested in a CLI/terminal-style product
- The operator does not have or want crypto exposure

## Objections and Responses

**"Public.com already does this — why Gordon?"**
"Public.com does agentic US equities trading. Gordon does agentic trading across crypto, stocks, and onchain with a permission model that lets you define exactly how much control you delegate. If your entire portfolio is US equities on Public.com's platform, Public.com might be fine. If you have Coinbase, a Schwab account, and a Solana wallet, Gordon is the only product that can see and operate across all three."

**"Public.com is backed by real investors and is a regulated brokerage. Isn't that safer?"**
"Public.com's regulatory status covers their custodial US equities business. Gordon connects to your existing accounts at Schwab, Alpaca, Binance — venues you already trust and already have assets at. You don't move assets to Gordon. Gordon works with what you already have."

**"Public.com has more users and more social proof."**
"True. They also launched one month ago with a single venue. The question is whether agentic trading should be built around one venue with opaque execution, or around your full portfolio with a permission model you control. We think the answer is your portfolio, your rules."

## Key Vulnerability

Public.com's "Agentic Brokerage" framing collapses if an AI-driven trade goes wrong and operators cannot explain why it happened. Their lack of a plan-first preview and permission model creates a single catastrophic failure mode: one bad trade that the operator didn't see coming destroys trust in the entire category for their user base. This is Gordon's window — be the product that shows operators what's about to happen before it happens.

---

## Churn Signals (Public.com → Gordon)

Watch for operators who:
- Have Public.com but also have crypto accounts that aren't connected
- Post on Twitter/Reddit about an AI trade they didn't understand or didn't intend
- Use Public.com for stocks but continue manual trading for crypto separately
- Complain that Public.com's agent "did something I didn't approve"
- Ask "how do I tell it to stop" or "can I set limits on what it does"

These operators have validated that they want AI-assisted execution. They are ready for Gordon's permission model.
